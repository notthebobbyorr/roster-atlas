Model structure & feature importance

From process skills to fantasy grades

Measured MLB skills + translated minor-league skills + estimates from official outcomes → next-season skill forecasts → category rates → park scenario → standard workload → fixed-reference SD grades.

Skill forecasts use the prior three seasons, sample reliability, recent changes, age, age squared, and age × existing skill. Missing birth dates use age 27. Each skill has its own regularized model; limited history receives more weight on its observed translated baseline. Outcome-only estimates fill missing inferable KPIs with added uncertainty; physical tracking measurements such as EV90 are not fabricated.

The category mappings are exposure-weighted ridge regressions fitted to MLB outcomes through 2026. Features are centered at their training medians and divided by their training standard deviations. Missing features use the training median; weak input coverage produces an unavailable prediction.

How the categories are assembled

  • Power: modeled HR per AB × 600 AB.
  • AVG: (1 − strikeouts per AB) × hits per contacted AB, with hits at least as high as HR. Contact risk and contact quality are modeled separately.
  • Speed: opportunities per AB × forecast takeoff rate × pooled prior-season steal-success rate × 600 AB. Takeoff is a separate forecast, so the opportunity-model weights below are only part of Speed.
  • K: modeled strikeouts per IP × role workload.
  • ERA: 9 × modeled earned runs per IP. WHIP: modeled hits per IP + walks per IP. Park adjustments use HR and non-HR-hit components.

Park scenarios combine 50% selected home park and 50% neutral road. Workloads are 600 AB / 175 SP IP / 60 RP IP / 100 swingman IP. Contribution grades use excess hits, earned runs prevented, and baserunners prevented for the ratio categories. Lower ERA and WHIP become higher grades. Sum gives the three final grades equal weight.

Reading the fitted feature weights

These are actual final-model coefficients, ranked by absolute size within each component. A signed coefficient is the change in the component rate for a +1 training SD change in that input, holding the others fixed. The bar is that feature’s share of the sum of absolute coefficients in this component.

These shares describe model weights, not causal importance, explained variance, or a player’s personal contribution. Correlated features can share weight or have surprising signs. Shares cannot be compared across components or added into a final AVG, WHIP, or Speed importance score. Positive means a higher component rate: for strikeouts per AB, hits allowed, walks allowed, and earned runs allowed, that is unfavorable.

How to interpret this model

600 AB for hitters · 175 IP for starters · 60 IP for relievers · 100 IP for swingmen. These are comparison workloads, not forecasts of health or playing time. Role overrides change workload only.

Quality compares rates. Contribution includes volume: excess hits for AVG, earned runs prevented for ERA, baserunners prevented for WHIP, and category totals for HR, SB and K. The pitcher scale is shared across all three roles. Reference: preseason-eligible player profiles from held-out 2025/2026 forecasts, with at least 100 AB or 30 IP in the prior season. The scale stays fixed when filtering players, changing parks or overriding workload.

Each park scenario is 50% selected home park and 50% neutral road. Player contact shape and batting side inform separate HR and non-HR-hit effects; these feed AVG, ERA and WHIP. SB, K and walks have no park effect in this version. Park-factor estimation remains an approximation, not a causal guarantee.

Speed means SB contribution. It combines forecast takeoff rate, modeled opportunities and a pooled prior-season steal-success rate. It is not a pure running-speed measurement. R, RBI, wins and saves are deferred.

Outcome ranges show the middle 80% of held-out residuals translated to grade units. They include season-to-season luck and are not confidence intervals for true skill. Small changes in point grades should not be treated as certain differences.

Roster context is the configured October 3, 2026 snapshot. Players without a known team have no current-park grade; select a neutral or named park. Missing skills remain unavailable.

Held-out category validation

2025 and 2026 predictions use only earlier training outcomes. Negative RMSE difference means the skill model beat a simple regressed-rate baseline. A positive difference is worse; standardizing the output does not change that.
