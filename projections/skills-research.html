Playing-time model, weights & validation
Model structure & feature importance

Bayesian feature model · experimental comparison. Pitcher K adds launch-angle bins, arsenal traits, usage rates and usage interactions. ERA and WHIP remove the joint Damage allowed / launch angle ≤0° block. Removing correlated inputs is not a causal claim about damage. New physical traits use measured MLB data only, shrink toward same-season MLB priors, and receive zero standardized prior when unmeasured; no invented MiLB physical measurements.

Walk-forward errors hold each displayed feature set fixed and choose recency weights using earlier folds. Feature sets were selected after reviewing the full experiment, so these errors are not independent validation of feature selection. See the research report for chronological feature-selector results.

Forecast relative skill, then translate it to a league environment

Actual MLB and translated MiLB records → regressed seasonal outcome lines and process skills → annual standardized inputs → three calendar-season weights → age adjustment → forecast category SD → assumed league environment → park scenario → standard workload.

The primary forecast is a player's category strength in standardized units relative to the MLB outcome population. Higher is better in every category; ERA and WHIP reverse the rate direction. Regressed, park-neutral MLB outcome lines are the targets. Adding minor-league profiles does not change the MLB population used to calculate the annual baselines.

Each category has its own recency weights. The hard constraint is n > n−1 > n−2, with nonnegative weights summing to 100%. The three calendar slots keep their weights when data are missing. Actual MLB or translated MiLB data take precedence; the most recent missing season uses an MLB-equivalent AAA baseline and the next missing season uses AA. These baseline inputs and regression ballast add no observed playing time or independent evidence.

Evaluation covers 2018, 2019, and 2021–2026, each with at least three preceding input seasons. The shortened 2020 season is excluded from the evaluation results. Coefficient training and evaluation require 200 actual AB for hitters or 40 actual IP for pitchers. Harmonic weights combine actual input-window PA/TBF with actual target-season PA/TBF: 2 × input × target / (input + target). Ballast is excluded from both exposures. Sparse players can still receive forecasts below these outcome cutoffs.

MASE divides absolute standardized forecast error by historical mean absolute year-to-year change in standardized outcomes. That naïve scale uses only earlier qualifying consecutive seasons and excludes pairs touching 2020. NRMSE divides standardized RMSE by the weighted SD of the standardized targets. Lower is better in both metrics.

Recency uses an ordered generalized Bayesian posterior initialized around 50/33.3/16.7, with equal normalized MASE and NRMSE loss and chronologically calibrated learning rate. Historical forecasts and their age adjustments use only earlier information. Fold-specific weight choices use earlier validation results; future folds do not tune earlier forecasts. The final 2027 weights use all eight completed evaluation seasons. Their retrospective fit is reported separately from rolling selection's out-of-sample results. Overall errors pool player-season observations with harmonic weights; MASE uses each fold's historical naïve scale and NRMSE uses the pooled target SD.

Converting 2027 SD to the card's real units

For each category, the assumed 2027 league mean and SD are weighted averages of the preceding three seasons using that category's selected recency weights. Means use actual park-neutral MLB league rates; SDs use the regressed MLB player outcome spread. Neutral rate = assumed mean + forecast SD × assumed SD; subtract for ERA and WHIP because lower rates are better. This is an explicit environment assumption, not an independent forecast of future league rules, baseballs, or weather. A new environment can change the raw rates while leaving the standardized skill forecast intact.

Park adjustments come after the neutral forecast and combine 50% selected home park with 50% neutral road. Player cards convert the adjusted rates back to the same 2027 scale. Workloads remain 600 AB / 175 SP IP / 60 RP IP / 100 swingman IP. Rate quality is the primary grade. The contribution view also accounts for the comparison workload; role overrides change volume, not the standardized rate forecast. R, RBI, wins, and saves remain deferred.

Reading the fitted feature weights

These are coefficients of the standardized category models. A coefficient is the change in the favorable-direction category SD forecast for a +1 annual SD change in that process input, holding the other inputs fixed. Positive coefficients improve the category forecast, including ERA and WHIP. Inputs retain their own natural direction: more damage allowed, for example, is a larger input value. The table does not turn an unfavorable input into a favorable one.

The bar is each feature's share of the category's sum of absolute coefficients. These are fitted associations, not causal effects, explained variance, or a complete attribution for an individual player. Correlated inputs can share weight or have unexpected signs. Shrinkage, surface anchors, age adjustments, clipping, and park effects can alter the final response.

How to interpret this model

600 AB for hitters · 175 IP for starters · 60 IP for relievers · 100 IP for swingmen. These are comparison workloads, not forecasts of health or playing time. Role overrides change workload only.

Quality compares rates. Contribution includes volume: excess hits for AVG, earned runs prevented for ERA, baserunners prevented for WHIP, and category totals for HR, SB and K. The pitcher scale is shared across all three roles. Reference: preseason-eligible player profiles from held-out 2025/2026 forecasts, with at least 100 AB or 30 IP in the prior season. The scale stays fixed when filtering players, changing parks or overriding workload.

Each park scenario is 50% selected home park and 50% neutral road. Player contact shape and batting side inform separate HR and non-HR-hit effects; these feed AVG, ERA and WHIP. SB, K and walks have no park effect in this version. Park-factor estimation remains an approximation, not a causal guarantee.

Speed means SB contribution. It combines forecast takeoff rate, modeled opportunities and a pooled prior-season steal-success rate. It is not a pure running-speed measurement. R, RBI, wins and saves are deferred.

Outcome ranges show the middle 80% of held-out residuals translated to grade units. They include season-to-season luck and are not confidence intervals for true skill. Small changes in point grades should not be treated as certain differences.

Roster context is the configured October 3, 2026 snapshot. Players without a projected MLB role use neutral park when Current home park is selected. Choose a named park to explore a move. Added minor-league profiles do not change the fixed MLB reference or run environment. Profiles without usable 2024–2026 inputs remain unavailable. Missing skills remain unavailable.

Category validation and recency selection

2025 and 2026 predictions use only earlier training outcomes. Negative RMSE difference means the skill model beat a simple regressed-rate baseline. A positive difference is worse; standardizing the output does not change that.
